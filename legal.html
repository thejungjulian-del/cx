Terms of use

  • Play money only. Your $10,000 starting balance, your shares and your dividends have no cash value and can't be withdrawn, sold or transferred outside CX.
  • Things may be reset. This is a preview. Balances, listings and accounts may be changed or reset while CX is being built.
  • Not advice. Valuations, model values and research are estimates for learning and discussion. They are not investment, tax or legal advice.
  • Be yourself. List a creator only if you are that creator or have their written permission. Don't impersonate anyone. CX confirms identity with a code in the creator's bio before any listing goes live.
  • Play fair. No automated trading, no attempts to break or overload the site, and no using several accounts to move prices. CX can remove accounts and listings that break these rules.
  • 18 and older. You need to be at least 18 to open an account, and you give your date of birth when you do.
  • As is. CX is provided as is, without warranties. Example listings, their price histories and part of their IPO demand are simulated.

Risks

These apply to CX today as a simulation, and they're the risks a real version would carry:

  • Profit can turn to losses. A creator can stop posting, lose sponsors, get demonetized or lose an account. Dividends stop when there's no profit, and you can lose everything you paid.
  • It depends on one person. A creator's revenue usually depends on them personally: their health, reputation and interest in continuing.
  • Platforms change the rules. YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and Twitch change how they pay creators with little notice.
  • You may not be able to sell. Trading needs someone on the other side. Prices can move fast, and a real version would have a waiting period before resale.
  • Estimates are not earnings. CX Research figures come from public stats and assumptions. They can be far from what a creator actually earns.
  • The creator is the business. If a creator quits, trading stops and shareholders are left with a slice of whatever the business still owns, which may be little.

The path to real money

Selling shares in a creator's business to the public is a securities offering under U.S. law. Here's how CX plans to do it properly before any real money is involved:

StepWhat it takes
OfferingsEach creator's IPO runs under Regulation Crowdfunding (up to $5 million per creator every 12 months) through an SEC-registered funding portal or broker-dealer, with a Form C filing and financial statements reviewed or audited by an independent accountant, depending on size. Larger creators could use Regulation A.
Investor limitsUnder Regulation Crowdfunding, people who aren't accredited investors can only invest a limited amount each year, based on their income and net worth.
TradingShares bought in a crowdfunding offering generally can't be resold for one year. After that, trading must run on an alternative trading system operated by a registered broker-dealer.
Identity & moneyIdentity checks, anti-money-laundering screening and payments handled by licensed providers.
ReportingOngoing annual reports for each creator, which is what CX's quarterly CX-Q and yearly CX-K are designed around.

This is a plan, not legal advice. CX will work with securities counsel and a registered partner before taking real money.

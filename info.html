The stock market for creators.

Fans buy shares in a creator's business and get a dividend from its profit every quarter. Creators keep control and can buy their shares back anytime.

In short

  1. 1A creator lists their business and sells shares to fans in an IPO.
  2. 2Every quarter, part of the business's profit is paid to shareholders as a dividend. The business is everything the creator earns, from every platform, sponsors and merch.
  3. 3There's no end date. After the 12-month legal hold, fans can sell their shares to other fans.
  4. 4The creator can buy every share back anytime at the 90-day average price plus 20%.
  5. 5Raises would run through an SEC-registered portal. Today CX is a play-money demo you can try.

The problem

Creators run businesses that can't raise money.

$250Bcreator economy in 2023, heading toward $480B by 2027
4%of creators earn more than $100,000 a year
46%of full-time creators earned under $1,000 in 2022

Goldman Sachs Research, 2023 · Linktree survey via TechCrunch, 2022

Banks don't lend against views. So a creator who wants to grow pays for it themselves or gives a studio part of the rights. Fans already spend on memberships, merch and crowdfunding, but they get rewards, never a return.

How it works

Shares in a creator, paid out every quarter.

  1. 1
    The creator lists their business. They choose how much of the company to sell, for example 10%, and what share of profit to pay out, at least half.
  2. 2
    Fans buy shares at the IPO through an SEC-registered crowdfunding portal. The money goes to the creator.
  3. 3
    Every quarter, the creator reports revenue, costs and profit. The promised share of profit is paid to shareholders as a dividend.
  4. 4
    After the 12-month legal hold, shares trade on CX through a registered partner, so prices move as the creator grows.
  5. 5
    The creator can buy every share back anytime at the 90-day average price plus 20%. If they miss two quarterly reports, they have to.

Creators and fans

Both sides know the deal up front.

Creators

Get

  • Money up front while keeping most of the company and all creative control
  • Fans with a stake who want the channel to grow
  • The right to buy shares back anytime

Give

  • Part of the profit every quarter until they buy the shares back
  • A quarterly report, and a limit on their own pay set in the charter

Fans

Get

  • A dividend from the creator's profit every quarter, growing as the creator grows
  • Shares they can sell after 12 months, and 20% above the average price if the creator buys them back

Risk

  • Dividends drop if profit drops, a loss pays nothing, and they can lose money
  • No selling for the first 12 months

The legal path

Every raise would run through an SEC-registered Regulation Crowdfunding portal, which handles investor checks, limits and escrow. The creator's company is set up as a corporation, so fans get a normal dividend tax form instead of partnership tax forms. The creator's own pay is capped in the charter, so profit can't be moved into salary. A lawyer will confirm the setup. Shares can't be resold for 12 months. After that, trading runs through a registered partner.

What's built

A full play-money exchange. It has an order book, IPO auctions, quarterly filings and dividends, creator buybacks, a brokerage-style account opening with a risk quiz, and automated tests that click through every trade.

The stock market for creators.

Fans buy shares in a creator's business and get a cut of their revenue every quarter. Creators keep control and can buy their shares back anytime.

In short

  1. 1A creator lists their business and sells shares to fans in an IPO.
  2. 2Every quarter, each share pays a cut of the creator's revenue from every platform, sponsors and merch.
  3. 3There's no end date. After the 12-month legal hold, fans can sell their shares to other fans.
  4. 4The creator can buy shares back anytime at the market price plus 10%.
  5. 5Raises run through an SEC-registered portal. Today CX is a play-money demo you can try.

The problem

Creators run businesses that can't raise money.

$250Bcreator economy in 2023, heading toward $480B by 2027
4%of creators earn more than $100,000 a year
46%of full-time creators earned under $1,000 in 2022

Goldman Sachs Research, 2023 · Linktree survey via TechCrunch, 2022

Banks don't lend against views. So a creator who wants to grow pays for it themselves or gives a studio part of the rights. Fans already spend on memberships, merch and crowdfunding, but they get rewards, never a return.

How it works

Shares in a creator, paid out every quarter.

  1. 1
    The creator lists their business. They choose what cut of revenue to share, for example 3%, and how many shares to sell.
  2. 2
    Fans buy shares at the IPO through an SEC-registered crowdfunding portal. The money goes to the creator.
  3. 3
    Every quarter, the creator reports revenue and a payment agent pays shareholders their cut first.
  4. 4
    After the 12-month legal hold, shares trade on CX through a registered partner, so prices move as the creator grows.
  5. 5
    The creator can buy shares back anytime at the market price plus 10%.

Creators and fans

Both sides know the deal up front.

Creators

Get

  • Money up front without selling the company or creative control
  • Fans with a stake who want the channel to grow
  • The right to buy shares back anytime

Give

  • A cut of revenue every quarter until they buy the shares back
  • A short quarterly revenue report

Fans

Get

  • A cut of a creator's revenue every quarter, growing as the creator grows
  • Shares they can sell after 12 months, or sell back at the market price plus 10%

Risk

  • Payouts drop if revenue drops, and they can lose money
  • No selling for the first 12 months

The legal path

Every raise runs through an SEC-registered Regulation Crowdfunding portal, which handles investor checks, limits and escrow. Shares are revenue shares, set up so fans shouldn't get partnership tax forms, which a lawyer will confirm. Shares can't be resold for 12 months. After that, trading runs through a registered partner.

What's built

A full play-money exchange. It has an order book, IPO auctions, quarterly filings and payouts, a brokerage-style account opening with a risk quiz, and automated tests that click through every trade. Try it.
